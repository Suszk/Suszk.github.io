Problem

Commuting costs too much, and carpooling feels unsafe with strangers.

Zatch

A live shopping app where buyers watch, bargain and buy in real time, like an Indian bazaar but online.

What I did

Designed the app and built the Zatch brand, from research to pitch.

A live, bargain-first app with an MVP planned for Q1 2026.

4 min read
MethodsMarket researchCompetitive analysisJourney mappingPositioning mapBrand building

The problem

Buyers
  • Just photos and text, so it's hard to trust
  • Can't ask, hear or interact
  • No fun, just transactions
Sellers
  • Drowned out by big brands
  • Can't pitch like in a real market
  • Forced to cut prices; buyers remember the platform, not them
01ResearchWhat do people need?

Today's journey is broken

Buying from a seller's reel today takes 8 steps:

  1. Sees a reel
  2. Takes a screenshot
  3. Sends a DM
  4. Waits hours or days
  5. Seller checks stock by hand
  6. Pays by QR code
  7. Types address in chat
  8. No tracking

Highlighted steps are where buyers lose trust or give up.

Competitive analysis

Amazon / Flipkart

Strong: huge reach, trusted checkout and delivery. Amazon brought back influencer livestreams for its 2023 festival sale.

Zatch's edge: small sellers aren't buried, and buyers can bargain instead of paying a fixed price.

Meesho

Strong: 0% seller commission and a focus on small sellers and value shoppers. It earns from shipping, ads and creator content fees.

Zatch's edge: real-time bargaining and seller-led live selling. Zatch can't win on fees, so it has to win on experience.

Instagram / WhatsApp

Strong: where small sellers already have followers, and it's free. But Facebook ended live shopping in 2022, and Instagram dropped product tags in live streams in 2023.

Zatch's edge: keeps the social feel but replaces screenshots, DMs and QR codes with one tap to buy.

Whatnot (US)

Strong: proves live shopping works at scale, built around live auctions. It charges about 8% plus a payment fee.

Zatch's edge: one-to-one bargaining fits Indian habits better than auctions, with UPI payments built in.

02DefineWhat's the real problem?

Where Zatch sits

What Zatch must get right

Live shopping has struggled in India before: Bulbul was acquired, YouTube's Simsim shut down, Moj scaled back.

  • Start with sellersBring sellers who already have followers, like the planned Hyderabad beta with 50+ sellers.
  • Bigger basketsBargaining and bundles to lift order value above a few dollars.
  • Control deliveryBuilt-in shipping and tracking to keep costs and returns down.
03IdeateWhat could solve it?

The idea

Bring the Indian bazaar online. Live, interactive and trusted: watch the product, talk to the seller, bargain, and buy without leaving the app.

Unique selling point

Watch it live. Bargain for it. Buy it in one tap.

Of the apps compared, only Zatch does all three.

  • Bargaining built inA cultural habit no marketplace has made a product feature.
  • Trust you can seeLive video, token-based orders and tracking replace DMs and QR codes.
  • A stage for small sellersTheir own storefront and live shows, so buyers remember them.
04DesignWhat does it look like?

Brand building

  • TaglineShop live. Bargain real. Buy instantly.
  • A brand word"Zatching", so people say "Let's Zatch".
  • Bold lookElectric lime and black, loud like a bazaar.
  • Playful detailsHand-drawn scribbles and stickers.

Key features

For buyers
  • Live feed of shows and short product reels
  • Zatching: bargain with the seller in real time
  • Instant UPI checkout, no redirects
  • Zatch AI answers questions and suggests a fair price
  • Order tracking in one place
For sellers
  • Own storefront, their "online dukaan"
  • Go live and sell to viewers
  • Buy Bits: shoppable short reels
  • Dashboard for stock, orders and payments
  • Built-in shipping with tracking

Signature feature: Zatching

Agree on a price, close the deal. Bargaining, built in.

Catch the sellerMatch the priceSnatch the deal
06DeliverWhat changed?

Business model

  • 8–12%Commission, below big marketplaces
  • AdsPaid promotions for sellers
  • VideoMaking product videos for sellers
  • FintechSmall fee on shipping and payments

Roadmap

  1. Q1 2026MVP for buyers and sellers
  2. Q2Closed beta with 50+ Hyderabad sellers
  3. Q3Shipping partners, new categories and regions
  4. Q4India-wide launch, AI personalisation, local languages

The pitch

The research became a 16-slide investor deck.

Social media

I run Zatch's Instagram, @zatch.shop. The voice is playful and a little cheeky, built around the brand's lime green: launch teasers for Zatch Hours, bargaining jokes and festive posts.

01 / 10The hookShop live. Bargain real. Buy instantly.

What I learned

  • Culture is a featureBargaining isn't a bug to remove. It's why people shop.
  • Trust beats priceLive video, verified orders and tracking fix what DMs can't.
  • Serve both sidesA marketplace only works if sellers win too.

"Not found" means the feature didn't show up in public descriptions of that site. It may still exist.

India's national portal (cybercrime.gov.in)

Does well: a guided step-by-step report, evidence upload, anonymous reporting, a reference number to follow up, and reports on suspicious numbers, links and social handles.

For TGCSB: send people straight to the right form on this portal instead of making them start over.

Maharashtra Cyber

Does well: lets citizens report by helpline, website or app, backed by a command centre and awareness programmes.

For TGCSB: a mobile-first experience matters, since many victims report from their phones right after a scam.

Report Fraud, UK (replaced Action Fraud in December 2025)

Does well: puts phone and online reporting side by side and explains what to report and how the information is used.

For TGCSB: add a short "what happens after you report" section to reduce anxiety and repeat calls.

RCMP and Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (launched November 2025)

Does well: anonymous reports, evidence upload, and reports from witnesses and businesses, not just victims.

For TGCSB: add "Saw a scam? Report it", so people who spot fraud can warn the police before anyone loses money.

cyber.gov.au, Australia (our benchmark)

Does well: opens with "Affected by a cyber issue?" and Report / Recover buttons. It runs a 1300 CYBER1 hotline, gives advice by audience, labels alerts by severity, and lets you check a report with its number.

For TGCSB: the model for putting help first. Next step: a "Recover" guide for after the report.

Brand idea

The bazaar, live. Shopping should feel like a conversation, not a checkout.

Colour

Electric lime#C9F26B · energy, deals, highlights
Ink#111111 · headlines, buttons
White#FFFFFF · space to breathe

Lime is for one thing per screen: the deal or the action. Never for body text.

Type

Headlines

HEAVY, CAPS, SHORT

A heavy display face, set tight. Three to five words.

Body

Clean and friendly sans for everything people read.

Regular weight, sentence case, generous line height.

Voice

  • Playful"Let's Zatch" over "Make an offer".
  • DesiBazaar words people already use.
  • ConfidentShort claims, no hedging.
  • ClearPrices and steps always plain.

Do and don't

DoDon't
"Zatch it for ₹900?""Submit a price negotiation request"
Use Zatch as a verb: "Let's Zatch"Write "zatch" in lowercase or as "ZATCH" in body text
One lime highlight per screenLime backgrounds behind long text

Graphic details

Hand-drawn scribbles, stickers and real phone screens keep it human. No stock 3D shapes.

311 Toronto website: UX audit

An expert review of the City's main help page, where residents report problems and track requests.

Heuristic evaluationAccessibilityUX recommendations
10heuristics checked
4clear task buttons
180+languages supported
3fixes proposed

Scope

Reviewed the 311 Toronto landing page on desktop against Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics, November 2026.

Expert review only. The next step is a 5-person Maze test with Toronto residents.

What works

  • Clear tasksSubmit, Track, Find information and Explore your neighbourhood are big, plain buttons.
  • InclusiveHelp in 180+ languages, 24/7, plus text-size controls.
  • Two ways inSearch by keyword or by request number.

Issues found

IssueHeuristicSeverity
"Always call 911 for emergencies" sits in small footer textError preventionHigh
Two search boxes on one page: site search and 311 searchConsistencyMedium
Trending links are long strings joined by dots, hard to scanMinimalist designMedium
The 311 mobile app link is buried under Related informationFlexibilityLow

Recommendations

  1. Emergency firstA red "Emergency? Call 911" bar above the tasks
  2. One searchMerge into a single box that understands request numbers
  3. Scannable trendsShort topic chips instead of dotted strings
  4. Test itMaze first-click test with 5 residents

Content strategy · Plain language

TGCSB homepage: plain-language rewrite

Rewriting the bureau's introduction so a scam victim knows what to do in one read.

UX writingPlain languageSEO
16 → 64Flesch reading ease
22 → 12words per sentence
1930call to action added
3search terms targeted

Before

"The Telangana Cyber Security Bureau (TGCSB) stands as the state's dedicated leader in combating and preventing cybercrime. Our mission is to create a secure digital environment through transparent, unbiased, and efficient practices supported by cutting-edge technology and advanced forensic expertise. We work in close coordination with national and international cybersecurity agencies to uphold the principle that 'data may be manipulated, but evidence speaks for itself.'"

After

"The Telangana Cyber Security Bureau (TGCSB) is the state police unit that stops cybercrime and helps victims. If you lose money to a scam, call 1930 right away. The faster you call, the better the chance we can freeze the money. We use modern tools and digital forensics to trace criminals. We also work with police and agencies across India and abroad."

What changed

  • Action firstThe 1930 helpline is now in the second sentence.
  • ShorterSentences cut from 22 to 12 words on average.
  • Plain words"Stops cybercrime" instead of "combating and preventing".
  • Why it mattersExplains that calling fast helps freeze the money.

Search terms targeted

report cyber crime Telanganacyber fraud helpline 1930TGCSB

Reading ease measured with the Flesch formula: 16 is "very hard", 64 is "plain English".

Concept · Storyboard

HopIn: Emma's commute, in six frames

A storyboard built from the HopIn research, so the team could see the problem as a story before looking at screens.

StoryboardPersonaResearch synthesis
6frames
1persona: Emma, 28
2moments that decide it: trust and time

The story

  1. FRAME 17:40 am, againEmma's bus is full. The next one is 20 minutes away.
  2. FRAME 2Late, every weekTransit takes almost twice as long as driving. She's stressed before work starts.
  3. FRAME 3Someone mentions HopInShe checks the app. A neighbour drives her route at 7:50.
  4. FRAME 4Can she trust a stranger?Verified profiles, reviews and a shared trip status help her say yes.
  5. FRAME 5The first rideBooked in a few taps. The driver arrives on time, and the cost is split.
  6. FRAME 6She keeps using itShe rates the ride and books the same driver for next week.

What it showed

  • Trust comes firstFrame 4 is where people drop off. Commuters told us trust mattered more than price.
  • Time is the triggerThe switch starts with a late bus, not a discount.
  • Habit is the goalRebooking the same driver turns one ride into a routine.
